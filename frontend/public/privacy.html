Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026

RabbitHole builds Spotify playlists of new music based on an artist you choose. This page explains what information RabbitHole uses, why, and how you can delete it. RabbitHole is not affiliated with or endorsed by Spotify or Last.fm.

What RabbitHole gets from Spotify

  • Your Spotify account ID. This is used to keep your history separate from other people's. RabbitHole does not ask for your email address or other profile details.
  • Permission to create playlists. RabbitHole only creates a playlist when you click "Create on Spotify". Playlists are created as private. RabbitHole cannot read your listening history.
  • A Spotify login token that keeps you connected. It is stored encrypted, and the key that unlocks it is never stored on the server.

What RabbitHole stores

  • For each playlist you create: its name, the seed artist, the number of tracks, its Spotify link, and when it was created. This is the history you see on the page.
  • The songs used in those playlists (song name, artist name, and Spotify link), so later playlists can avoid repeating them.
  • In your browser: a session token in local storage that keeps you signed in on that device.
  • Server logs, which can include your IP address, your Spotify account ID, and the artists you search for. These are used to run the service, prevent abuse, and fix problems.

Services RabbitHole uses

  • Spotify, to sign you in, search for music, and create playlists on your account.
  • Last.fm, to find similar artists and popular songs. Only artist and song names are sent to Last.fm, never your identity.
  • Sentry, if error alerts are turned on, which receives technical details about errors. Error reports do not include your IP address or login details.
  • Hosting and database providers, which store the information above: add your hosting and database providers.

RabbitHole does not sell your information, show ads, or email you.

How long information is kept

  • Your playlist history and used songs are kept until you clear them or disconnect.
  • Login sessions are deleted when you disconnect, or after 30 days without use.

Your choices

  • Clear History deletes your playlist history and the list of used songs.
  • Disconnect deletes your history and used songs and signs you out on every device.
  • You can also remove RabbitHole's access from your Spotify account page. If you do, RabbitHole deletes your data the next time it tries to use that access. You can also contact us below to have it deleted straight away.
  • Playlists already created on your Spotify account stay there until you delete them in Spotify.

Children

RabbitHole is not intended for children under 13.
